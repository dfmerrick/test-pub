Semester Intelligence Project (SIP) 4 - Final Product

Assignment Summary

This assignment is the conclusion of the Semester Intelligence Project (SIP).

This assignment is two-fold. The first portion will develop the Key Judgements and the Scope of the project. The second portion will assemble all pieces of the SIP into a single product.

Instructions

Using SIP 1, SIP 2, and SIP 3, the analyst will focus on what has been discovered, the analyst’s confidence in that discovered information, and the appropriate scope of the SIP as a whole. With this new product in hand, the student analyst will assemble a completed SIP report. The student analyst is encouraged to incorporate edits from the initial submissions of SIP 1, SIP 2, and SIP 3 into the final product.

Part 1 | Scope and Key Judgements: The analyst will consider all of the information and analysis conducted to date to outline Key Judgements (the bottom line findings or recommendations) and the Scope of the completed SIP. Key Judgements must include confidence levels.  Part 1 should be single-spaced, in 12pt Times New Roman or Calibri font.

Part 1, Section 1: The Scope of the SIP should be brief. The analyst will outline the target audience of the final SIP, based on the assigned topic and recipients. The student analyst may go beyond the initial audience if they deem it necessary or appropriate. The goal is to answer the question, “Who should read and understand this report?” Consider decision-makers, government officials, the general public, etc. but the audience should be specific - not simply ‘decision makers’.

The Scope should also include a very brief description of the topic, current situation, how the information that was collected projects development based on the analysis, and what all of this means to the target audience.

This section is similar to an abstract. It should be written last as it incorporates all of your research.

Part 1, Section 2:  The Key Judgements are bulleted statements that outline what has been concluded in this product. They should include a one-sentence judgment, further elaboration on ‘why’ and what that means for the report’s audience, and the analyst’s evaluation of their confidence in the judgment. Key Judgements should total three sentences each. The confidence level in this judgement is the analyst’s own judgement, base it on the analysis, but there is no wrong answer.

Below is an example of what key judgements should look like:

  • The author asserts with moderate confidence that Gryffindor will win the house cup. Analysis indicates that Dumbledore will award Harry, Ron, and Hermione enough points to tie with Slytherin House who previously held the lead in house points. He will then award Neville 10 more points, naming Gryffindor House the winner of the House Cup.
  • The author asserts with high confidence that Gryffindor will be successful in future house cups. Analysis indicates that the group of friends (all in Gryffindor house) are able to help each other in a way that earns them points towards their ultimate successes. They will continue to do the right thing and earn points towards the House Cup.

Part 2 | Complete SIP: The student analyst will assemble SIP 1, SIP 2, SIP 3, and Part 1 of this assignment together. The student analyst may incorporate edits or updates to the final product.

The format below lists the items you will include, and where they come from. Note that some items appear out of order - it is not a simple merge of SIP 1, SIP 2, and SIP 3.  Once assembled, the analyst has created a complete product.

This product is structured as an intelligence product, yet conducted in an academic environment. If done correctly, it is a valid analysis of a real issue - even if that topic may appear frivolous. Much of what analysts do is develop their own questions and answer them.

The Sources page (number 8 below) must be in ICD 203/ICS 203-01 format, with appropriate superscript citations. This will be the heaviest task in SIP 4 - you must combine all your sources into a single product, and renumber the superscript citations in the complete body of the product. Instructors recommend completing Step 1 of this assignment, assembling everything in the correct order via cut/paste (leave the original superscript notions in place when doing so), compiling all the Sources Referenced pages into the end of the single document, then renumbering the superscript notions.

Analysts to not need to include word count information in SIP 4.

The final SIP, and what will be submitted for this assignment, should be in the following order:

  1. Title Page
  2. Scope (from this assignment)
  3. Key Judgements (from this assignment)
  4. Acknowledgement of Biases (from SIP 1)
  5. Background (from SIP 1)
  6. Written Analysis  (SIP 3)
  7. Qualitative Analysis table (SIP 2)
  8. Sources Page (compiled from SIP 1, SIP 2, and SIP 3)
  9. Search Strings and Operators (from SIP 2 and SIP 3)

Requirements

  • The Scope section of the submission should be between 200-300 words (for undergraduates) or 300-400 words (for graduates), single-spaced, 12pt, Times New Roman or Calibri font.
  • The submission includes at least three (3) Key Judgements (for undergraduates) or five (5) Key Judgements (for graduates).
  • Endnote citations are required. All citations must be in ICD 203/ICS 203-1 format.
  • The submission will include a title page.
  • The submission will include a header and a footer.  The header must include the title of the report. The footer must include a page number and analyst name.
  • The submission will include outside sources (in ICD endnote format) that support the statements made in the submission.

No-Go Criteria

Submissions will be assessed first on the following, before any other rubric criteria.  Any failures to meet the foundational requirements will result in an automatic grade of ‘zero’. Further grading of the assignment will not occur.

  • The submission includes citations in ICD 203/ISD 203-01 format. This must include superscript inline citations and a properly formatted works cited/references list.
  • The submission includes all required components.

Again, if the submission does not pass the ‘no-go’ criteria, it will receive an automatic grade of ‘zero’.

Feedback

Students will receive some feedback upon completion of grading. The primary method of feedback will be the grading rubric. Student analysts should contact the instructor if the grade assigned is unclear.
