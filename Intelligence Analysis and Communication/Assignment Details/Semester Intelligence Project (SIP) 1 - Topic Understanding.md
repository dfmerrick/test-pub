# Semester Intelligence Project (SIP) 1 - Topic Understanding

### Assignment Summary

This assignment is the first step in completing the Semester Intelligence Project. The purpose is to ensure that the student analyst understands the assigned topic and is prepared to proceed through the remainder of the project. 

This assignment is two-fold. The first portion will develop the student analyst’s assigned topic, laying the foundation for the SIP. The second portion will discuss implicit and explicit analyst bias and is critical to the integrity of the entire SIP. 
### Instructions
The student will prepare a brief paper with two major sections.  

##### Section 1: The student will prepare a high-level background of the assigned topic and focus on how their project will address the intelligence questions. 

This assignment is similar to an academic research paper. The student analyst must discuss any background information that is significant to the topic assigned and associated intelligence questions. The analyst should present a solid amount of research and support on the assigned topic. Some questions to consider when writing your background:

- How has the topic developed in recent months or years? What are the origins of the topic? Is there significant historical information required for understanding?
- Why should decision makers care about the topic and the product that the analyst will develop this semester?
- What is happening that concerns the topic currently? Are there potential bad outcomes? 
- Are there any internal or external factors that influence the topic?
- Is there ANYTHING else that may interest the target audience?

##### Section 2: The student analyst will produce a discussion of their biases and how they will be accounted for in research and writing throughout the SIP. 
This methodology should apply to this assignment and to all future work on this topic. This assessment of bias should focus on the assigned topic and include both explicit and implicit bias concerns. The goal is for the student analyst to understand where bias issues may arise, acknowledge them, and clearly define how those issues will be minimized. Intelligence and analytical products should always include this component.  

Student analysts should provide examples of bias (either explicit or implicit) and cited sources that indicate effective research on how these biases may impact writing.  

Some of the questions that the product should answer are:

- How could biases influence the field of intelligence?
- What are some common biases that may be linked to your topic?
- How are some of these common biases presented?
- How can biases in the media sway public opinion on your assigned topic?
- What are your biases? Everyone is biased.
- What steps will the analyst take to account for bias?

The student analyst will make an acknowledgement of any personal biases they may have about the assigned topic and the steps that they have taken to mitigate these. The analyst must be upfront here. The goal is for the instructors to clearly understand how the analyst will provide a final product that is unbiased.

The student analyst must not use first-person language. Statements such as “I will…” should be rephrased as “The analyst has…”, etc.  This applies to the entire Semester Intelligence Project.

This second section does not require citations, but the analyst may utilize cited information as necessary. 
### Requirements

- The submission should be between 900-1200 words (for undergraduates) or 1200-1500 words (for graduates), double-spaced, 12pt, Times New Roman or Calibri font. 
- The body should be fairly evenly split between the background content and the bias content.
- Endnote citations are required. All citations must be in ICD 203/ICS 203-1 format.
- The submission will include a title page. 
- The submission will include a header and a footer.  The header must include the word count of the body of the submission, which does not include the abstract or endnotes. The footer must include a page number and analyst name.
- The submission will include outside sources (in ICD endnote format)  that support the statements made in the submission.
### No-Go Criteria
Submissions will be assessed first on the following, before any other rubric criteria.  Any failures to meet the foundational requirements will result in an automatic grade of ‘zero’. Further grading of the assignment will not occur.  

- The submission includes citations in ICD 203/ISD 203-01 format. This must include superscript inline citations and a properly formatted works cited/references list.  
- The submission addresses the background of the assigned topic and establishes a foundation for the remainder of the Semester Intelligence Project.
- The submission includes a discussion of the student analyst’s potential bias.  

Again, if the submission does not pass the ‘no-go’ criteria, it will receive an automatic grade of ‘zero’.  
### Feedback

Students will receive feedback upon completion of grading. The primary method of feedback will be the grading rubric and instructor-provided comments, with emphasis on areas of deficiency. This feedback must be incorporated into later submissions in the SIP.  Student analysts should contact the instructor if the grade assigned is unclear.
