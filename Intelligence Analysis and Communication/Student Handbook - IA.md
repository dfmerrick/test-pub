## Course Learning Objectives

This course will expose students to techniques used in the analysis of information from across the globe in the production of intelligence including quantitative analysis, statistical analysis, data modeling, comparative analysis, data mining, data science, social networking analysis, and computational data analysis.

This course has been approved to meet **FSU’s Upper-Division Writing** requirement and helps you become a flexible and proficient writer for professional purposes.

In this course, you will compose as a process, including drafts, revisions, and editing. The writing cultivated by this process conforms to FSU’s definition of “college-level writing”, which is writing that:

- presents a clearly defined central idea or thesis;
- provides adequate support for that idea;
- is organized clearly and logically;
- is presented in a format appropriate to the purpose, occasion, and audience; and
- utilizes the conventions of a standard language.

As such, this course requires the completion of two or more substantial writing assignments or the equivalent. Instructors will provide criteria for evaluating your performance on writing, feedback on your writing (including instructor response), and opportunities for revision.

In order to fulfill FSU’s Upper-Division Writing requirement, the student must earn a “C-” or higher in the course, and earn at least a “C-” average on the required writing assignments. If the student does not earn a “C-” average or better on the required writing assignments, the student will not earn an overall grade of “C-” or better in the course, no matter how well the student performs in the remaining portion of the course.

This course has been approved to meet **FSU’s Liberal Studies Scholarship in Practice** requirement and helps you become a critical thinker, a creative user of knowledge, and an independent learner.

In order to fulfill FSU’s Scholarship in Practice requirement, the student must earn a “C-” or higher in the course.

By the end of this course, undergraduate students will be able to:

1. Explain the ethical dilemmas surrounding intelligence collection and analysis
2. Develop an intelligence report using the industry standard plain-language in a message-driven format
3. Analyze qualitative data using grounded research to create a theory generalizable to similar events
4. Prepare analyses and reports on both target and mission-specific topics
5. Operationalize, measure, and model intelligence based on quantitative data
6. Apply relevant areas of scholarship to produce an original project.
7. Evaluate appropriate evidence from multiple sources to illustrate how a chosen topic is relevant to a particular field.
8. Compose ideas clearly, coherently, and effectively for a particular purpose, occasion, or audience representative as appropriate for the field.

and graduate students will be able to....

9. Identify challenges in the current intelligence community within the scope of analytical functions
10. Explain the ethical dilemmas surrounding intelligence collection and analysis
11. Apply the industry standard plain-language in a message-driven format to create an intelligence report
12. Evaluate intelligence analyses based on characteristics and industry measurements
13. Analyze qualitative data using grounded research to create a theory generalizable to similar events
14. Synthesize tactical analyses and reports on both target and mission-specific topics

## Grading Policy

The final grade in this course will be calculated using three (3) categories: Assignments, Assessments, and the Semester Intelligence Project.

### Assignments

Assignments include classroom work, homework, and 60-second briefings. 

Assignments are worth 20% of your final grade.

### Assessments

There are three quizzes throughout the semester. These will be brief and based on a few weeks content. 

The critical thinking exam is ungraded, but should be treated as though it is a graded exam and taken seriously. While ungraded, completion of the critical thinking exam is required, and a completion grade will be assigned.

There is one 'real' exam - the final. The final will be essay-based and will be conducted on Canvas.

You should take thorough, well-organized notes throughout the semester.

Assessments are worth 40% of your final grade.

- Quiz 1
- Quiz 2
- Quiz 3
- Critical Thinking Test
- Final Exam - Document Review

### Semester Intelligence Project

Throughout the semester, you will complete four additive steps of your Semester Intelligence Project. The final report and presentation will be due at the end of the semester.

The [[Semester Intelligence Project (SIP)]] is worth 40% of your grade.

## Course Policies
### Late Work

Late work is not accepted in this course without a valid, university-approved excuse/documentation. Note that personal or family travel, sick pets, broken cars, internet problems, etc., do not constitute a valid excuse.
### Citations

Every written assignment that you turn in will require citations.

If there are exceptions to this rule, the assignment instructions will state that. If you are unsure if citations are required, you should assume that they are.

This source requirement is critical in both academic and professional settings. Intelligence writing requires precision and good sources. If you do not provide citations, how can a recipient of your work measure the effectiveness of the product?

All citations must be in ICD 206/ICS 206-1 format. This will be an unfamiliar format for students, and will be reviewed and discussed in class.

If you do not provide citations in the specified format, you will receive a grade of zero on the assignment. Citations mean both a references page and parenthetical citations in the body of your work. If you provide a references/works cited page and do not actually cite anything in the body of your work, you will get a zero.

Read the previous paragraph again. Seriously... you can provide a product that is amazing - and you will receive a zero without effective/proper citations.
### Grade Rounding

Final grades will be rounded to the nearest whole integer grade (a 92.2 becomes a 92, while a 92.5 becomes a 93), and the grading scheme will then be applied. No extra (freebie) points will be granted. Don't ask.
